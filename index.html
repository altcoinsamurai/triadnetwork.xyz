004   Reference Library

The campaigns that rewrote distribution.

Six campaigns from this decade that changed how attention gets earned. What they did, what we took from them, and where it shows up in the work we run for clients.

Liquid Death  ·  2024

Selling the opposite of health

Valued at $1.4bn in March 2024, on $263m of retail sales the year before. It is canned water.

The lesson

Identity outsells specification. Nobody bought it because of the water.

How we use it

We settle what a project stands for before we buy a single placement. A token with a worldview outlives one with a feature list.

Duolingo  ·  2021–

A mascot built for one platform

Passed 50 million daily active users in Q3 2025, up 36% year on year, with an owl that behaves like a creator rather than a logo.

The lesson

Speak a platform's native language instead of translating a brand voice onto it.

How we use it

Our creator briefs are written for CT, not adapted to it. Posts that read like ads get treated like ads.

Barbie  ·  2023

Saturation, executed in one window

$1.45bn worldwide at the box office. Mattel's Barbie billings rose 16% in the quarter around release.

The lesson

Saturation only works when every partner tells the same story at the same time. The film was the smallest part of it.

How we use it

Ecosystem launches move together or not at all. We sequence partners, creators and exchanges to land inside one window.

Spotify Wrapped  ·  Annual

Handing users their own data

Wrapped 2025 engaged more than 300 million users and produced over 630 million social shares.

The lesson

Give people something true about themselves and they will do the distribution for you.

How we use it

Onchain activity is the same raw material: personal, verifiable, and worth posting without being asked.

$73m → $402mAnnual sales, 2019 to 2022
Stanley Quencher  ·  2019–2023

A moment the brand did not start

A 110-year-old flask company grew from $73m to $402m in annual sales, driven by creators it had never hired.

The lesson

The brand's only job was to notice early and respond fast. It did not manufacture the moment.

How we use it

We watch for organic momentum and put budget behind it, rather than paying to invent it from a standing start.

4,000 clips, 90 secondsPrimetime Emmy, 2021
Nike, You Can't Stop Us  ·  2020

Craft as the argument

Thirty-six split-screen pairings cut from over 4,000 pieces of footage into ninety seconds. It won the 2021 Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Commercial.

The lesson

How hard something was to make is itself the message.

How we use it

In a market drowning in cheap content, production quality is a credibility argument before it is an aesthetic one.

None of these are Triad campaigns. They are the work we study and argue about, and the reasoning we bring to a client brief. Figures as reported by the companies, Box Office Mojo and the Television Academy.

005   Field Notes

The six rooms that set 2026’s agenda.

Where the industry argued this year, what it argued about, and what each room said about where capital and attention were actually moving. We read these as they happened, and they shape what we tell clients to build toward.

  1. 0110–12 Feb · Hong Kong · 11,000 from 122 countries

    Consensus Hong Kong

    Hong Kong used the stage to bid for Asia’s regulated hub status. Chief Executive John Lee promised the first stablecoin issuer licences within a month; the Financial Secretary simultaneously signalled only a small number would be granted, restricted to applicants with real use cases. It ran four days after a 15% single-session Bitcoin crash, so the institutional confidence on stage sat against visible panic in the market.

    The readThe licences slipped past Q1 and landed in April — on HSBC and Anchorpoint, the Standard Chartered and Animoca venture. Banks took Asia’s first regulated stablecoin slots, not crypto-native issuers. If you are building in APAC, your licensing path now runs through incumbents.
    zkMeAnchorpoint FinancialSolana Accelerate APAC
  2. 0217–21 Feb · Denver · Side events down ~68%

    ETHDenver

    The clearest measurable evidence that the builder economy had contracted. Side events fell from 668 the previous year to roughly 215, and the hackathon prize pool dropped from about $1.03m to $132,000. What budget remained concentrated almost entirely in AI — Sentient’s parallel Open AGI Summit was at times busier than the official main venue.

    The readA symptom, not a cause. Budgets had already been cut; ETHDenver simply measured it early and in public. The lesson for teams was blunt: sponsor-funded distribution stopped working this year, and the projects that held momentum were the ones that owned their own channels.
    SentientGensynPrismaXMonad
  3. 0327–29 Apr · Las Vegas · 500+ speakers

    Bitcoin 2026

    The year the mining industry publicly stopped being a mining industry. With Bitcoin near $79,000 against roughly $110,000 at the previous edition, and public miners’ Q1 cash cost reported at about $79,995 a coin, the economics had inverted. CleanSpark led its booth with “Optimizing Power Intelligence”; Bitdeer’s banner read “Bitcoin mining & AI Cloud.”

    The readThe event was the gap between the main stage and the floor — $10m-per-coin keynotes above booths with more staff than buyers. The miner-to-AI-datacentre rotation it made visible became the dominant Bitcoin-equity narrative for the rest of the year.
    CleanSparkBitdeerRiot Platforms
  4. 045–7 May · Miami Beach · ~15,000–20,000

    Consensus Miami

    The year crypto changed customer. JPMorgan held one of the largest booths on a floor that also carried S&P Global, DTCC, Mastercard, PayPal and Swift — nine years after its chief executive called Bitcoin worthless. Tokenised equities and stablecoin payment rails had replaced token sales, and the live argument was whether the industry had won by bending.

    The readRetail speculation had migrated to prediction markets, and token launches designed to dump on retail had got harder to run. If you are selling into this room, the buyer is a procurement committee and the pitch is settlement and compliance, not community.
    Canton NetworkKalshiOndoCrypto.com Travel
  5. 057–8 Oct · Singapore · 25,000 from 160 countries

    TOKEN2049 Singapore

    The largest crypto event of the year, and the AI-agent economy’s coming-out party — questioned from its own stage. Arthur Hayes launched FLOP, a payment network for AI agents, arguing agent commerce would drive blockchain payment demand. Hyperliquid’s Jeff Yan pushed back on the framing entirely. No speaker claimed agents were moving meaningful volume yet.

    The readIt marked the peak of the AI-agent narrative rather than launching a rally: October closed down 7.3%, Bitcoin’s weakest October since 2014. What did persist was tokenised equities — OKX and ICE filed to trade 63 US names, and Robinhood Chain took a session of its own.
    FLOPHyperliquidPolymarketRobinhood Chain
  6. 063–6 Nov · Mumbai · 364 sessions, nine tracks

    Devcon 8

    Ethereum reframed its roadmap around two threats — AI and quantum — and around India as its next developer base. Justin Drake warned that AI could break existing asymmetric encryption before quantum computers mature, turning post-quantum work from a distant hedge into a near-term deadline. Buterin’s answer for the base layer was deliberately conservative, hash-based cryptography.

    The readToo recent to judge; it closed a week ago. But the question nobody answered on stage is the one with commercial consequences: how AI agents holding wallets get integrated without blind trust in whoever wrote them.
    Lean EthereumKohakuTor Project

Triad neither organised nor sponsored these events. Figures are as reported by organisers and contemporary trade press; Consensus Miami attendance is disputed between the organiser’s 20,000 and a floor estimate of roughly 15,000, and Devcon has published no final count.
